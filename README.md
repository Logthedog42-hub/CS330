# CS330
1. How do I approach designing software?
Designing this scene totally changed how I think about building things. Instead of hardcoding every little object into place, I forced myself to break everything down into reusable chunks. I wrote individual functions for things like the book stack, candle, pumpkin, pilaster, and firebox, and then just pieced the whole room together from those functions. It saved me from going crazy trying to position everything manually. My workflow usually starts with picturing the end result, figuring out what objects I need, building each one out of basic shapes like boxes or cylinders, and leaving lighting and textures for last. Honestly, I used to think lighting was just a cosmetic finishing touch, but it completely changes how a shape reads. Taking the time to plan modular, reusable pieces upfront is a habit I'm definitely taking with me to future projects, whether it's graphics or standard app development.

2. How do I approach developing programs?
When it actually came to writing the code, I had to learn how to build things in layers. I’d get the basic geometry rendering on screen first, and only mess with textures, materials, and lighting once the shapes were sitting right. Organizing the code around clear PrepareScene and RenderScene functions saved me so many headaches when debugging. And man, iteration was brutal—almost nothing looked right on the first try. The flames took forever to look like actual fire until I layered two glowing cores, the pine cone geometry looked totally warped until I fixed it, and half my wreath leaves were hiding somewhere off-screen at first. Not to mention chasing down missing braces in the compiler errors. By the end of the project, my whole style shifted: instead of just hacking code together to get something to show up, I started working methodically—making one tiny change, compiling, checking the result, and repeating. It made tracking down bugs way faster.

3. How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?
Doing this computational graphics project really clicked a lot of things into place for me. Getting hands-on experience with 3D math, transformations, and lighting models gives me a much stronger foundation for upcoming classes like game development, simulation, or data visualization. Plus, wrestling with a bigger codebase and digging through documentation builds that real-world muscle memory. Professionally, even if I don't work exclusively in graphics, the engineering habits I picked up—breaking down hairy problems, working in tight feedback loops, and actually testing incremental changes—are universal. On top of that, having a fully rendered 3D scene that I built from scratch is a solid, tangible piece to show off to prospective employers.
